# WHO Essential Medical Guidelines Summary

## Cardiovascular Disease Prevention

### Primary Prevention
- **Blood pressure target**: <140/90 mmHg for general population; <130/80 mmHg for high-risk patients
- **Statin therapy**: Recommended for adults >40 years with 10-year CVD risk ≥10%
- **Aspirin**: NOT recommended for primary prevention in low-risk individuals; consider for high-risk
- **Smoking cessation**: Single most impactful cardiovascular risk reduction intervention
- **Physical activity**: 150-300 minutes moderate or 75-150 minutes vigorous activity per week

### Secondary Prevention (Post-MI/Stroke)
1. Antiplatelet therapy (dual for 12 months post-ACS, then single agent lifelong)
2. High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 40-80mg or rosuvastatin 20-40mg)
3. ACE inhibitor/ARB (especially if EF<40% or diabetes)
4. Beta-blocker (post-MI, especially if reduced EF)
5. Cardiac rehabilitation program

## Diabetes Management Guidelines (WHO/IDF 2023)

### Diagnosis Criteria
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two occasions
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during 75g OGTT
- HbA1c ≥6.5% (48 mmol/mol) on two occasions
- Random glucose ≥200 mg/dL with classic symptoms

### Management Targets
| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| HbA1c | <7.0% (individualize) |
| Fasting glucose | 80-130 mg/dL |
| Post-meal glucose | <180 mg/dL |
| Blood pressure | <130/80 mmHg |
| LDL cholesterol | <70 mg/dL (high risk) |
| BMI | <25 kg/m² |

### Pharmacotherapy Stepwise
1. **First line**: Metformin + lifestyle (if tolerated)
2. **Add-on**: SGLT2 inhibitor (if CVD/CKD) or GLP-1 RA (if obesity/CVD)
3. **Intensification**: DPP-4i, sulfonylurea, or basal insulin
4. **Insulin optimization**: Multiple daily injections or pump therapy

## Respiratory Infections

### Pneumonia (Community-Acquired)
**CURB-65 Score** (1 point each):
- Confusion (new)
- Urea >7 mmol/L (BUN >19 mg/dL)
- Respiratory rate ≥30/min
- Blood pressure <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic
- Age ≥65 years

**Management by score**:
- 0-1: Outpatient oral antibiotics (amoxicillin 1g TID x5d)
- 2: Inpatient consideration
- 3-5: Hospital/ICU admission

**First-line antibiotics**:
- Mild CAP: Amoxicillin 1g TID
- Atypical cover needed: Amoxicillin + clarithromycin
- Severe: Beta-lactam + macrolide or respiratory fluoroquinolone

### Upper Respiratory Tract Infections
- **Viral**: Symptomatic treatment only; antibiotics NOT indicated
- **Strep pharyngitis**: Penicillin V 500mg BID x10d or amoxicillin 500mg TID x10d
- **Sinusitis**: Usually viral; antibiotics only if bacterial features >10 days

## Mental Health First Aid

### Depression Recognition
**PHQ-9 Screening** (score 0-27):
- 5-9: Mild depression
- 10-14: Moderate depression  
- 15-19: Moderately severe depression
- ≥20: Severe depression

**First-line treatments**:
1. Psychological therapies (CBT first-line for mild-moderate)
2. Antidepressants (SSRIs: sertraline, escitalopram for moderate-severe)
3. Combination therapy for severe depression

**Safety assessment**: Always ask about suicidal ideation; refer urgently if active plan

### Anxiety Disorders
- GAD treatment: SSRIs/SNRIs + CBT (most effective combination)
- Panic disorder: SSRIs + CBT; benzodiazepines short-term only
- PTSD: Trauma-focused CBT or EMDR first-line

## Maternal and Child Health

### Antenatal Care Schedule (WHO 2016 Model - 8+ contacts)
- **Contact 1** (up to 12 weeks): Blood group, Rh, HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B, urine culture, folic acid, iron
- **Contact 2** (20 weeks): Anatomy scan, glucose screen
- **Contact 3** (26 weeks): OGTT if risk factors
- **Contact 4** (30 weeks): Blood pressure, fundal height, fetal heart tones
- **Contacts 5-8** (34-40 weeks): Frequent monitoring

### Danger Signs in Pregnancy
🚨 **Seek emergency care immediately for**:
- Severe headache + visual disturbance (preeclampsia)
- BP >160/110 (severe preeclampsia)
- Convulsions (eclampsia)
- Vaginal bleeding
- Severe abdominal pain
- Reduced fetal movements
- Fever + chills

## Emergency Medicine Protocols

### Basic Life Support (BLS) - Adult
1. **Scene safety** check
2. **Check responsiveness** - tap shoulders, shout
3. **Call 911** (or send someone)
4. **Check breathing** - no more than 10 seconds
5. **Begin CPR**: 30 compressions : 2 breaths
   - Rate: 100-120/min
   - Depth: At least 2 inches (5 cm)
   - Allow full chest recoil
6. **AED**: Attach as soon as available; minimize interruptions
7. Continue until EMS arrives, AED advises, or patient recovers

### ABCDE Emergency Assessment
- **A**irway: Ensure patent; jaw thrust if cervical injury
- **B**reathing: Rate, effort, SpO2; O2 if <94%
- **C**irculation: HR, BP, capillary refill, IV access
- **D**isability: GCS, pupils, blood glucose
- **E**xposure: Full exam, temperature, remove clothing safely

### Sepsis (Sepsis-3 Definition)
- **Sepsis**: Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection
- **SOFA score** ≥2 from baseline indicates organ dysfunction
- **Septic shock**: Sepsis + vasopressor requirement + lactate >2 mmol/L

**Hour-1 Bundle**:
1. Measure lactate
2. Blood cultures x2 before antibiotics
3. Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour
4. 30 mL/kg crystalloid IV bolus for hypotension/lactate >4
5. Vasopressors if hypotension persists
