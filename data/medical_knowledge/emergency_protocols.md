# Emergency Medical Protocols

## 🚨 Cardiac Arrest - CPR Protocol

### Recognition
- Unresponsive
- No normal breathing (no breathing or only gasping)
- No pulse (check carotid, max 10 seconds)

### Immediate Actions
1. Call 911 (or direct someone specific: "You in the red shirt, call 911 now!")
2. Start CPR immediately — do not wait for AED
3. Push hard and fast: 100-120 compressions/minute, ≥2 inches deep
4. Allow complete chest recoil between compressions
5. Minimize interruptions — aim for ≥60% chest compression fraction
6. If trained: 30 compressions : 2 rescue breaths
7. If untrained or unwilling: Hands-Only CPR (continuous compressions)
8. AED: Use as soon as available — attach, analyze, shock if advised

### Post-ROSC Care
- 12-lead ECG immediately — cath lab if STEMI
- Target SpO2 94-98%, PaCO2 35-45 mmHg
- Targeted temperature management if not following commands
- IV/IO access, vasopressors if needed (norepinephrine first-line)

## 🧠 Stroke Protocol — "Time is Brain"

### FAST Recognition
- **F**ace: Ask to smile — does one side droop?
- **A**rm: Ask to raise both — does one drift down?
- **S**peech: Ask to repeat a phrase — is it slurred or strange?
- **T**ime: If ANY yes — call 911 immediately

### Last Known Well Time
- Document precisely — determines tPA eligibility (4.5 hour window)
- Wake-up strokes: DWI-FLAIR mismatch on MRI may extend window

### Emergency Management
1. Call 911 — do NOT drive to hospital
2. Nothing by mouth (NPO)
3. CT head without contrast on arrival (rule out hemorrhage)
4. IV tPA if: ischemic, within 4.5 hours, no contraindications
5. Mechanical thrombectomy if: large vessel occlusion, within 24 hours
6. BP management: Allow up to 220/120 if no tPA; <185/110 if tPA given
7. Stroke unit admission, swallow assessment

## ⚡ Anaphylaxis Protocol

### Recognition (Two or more systems involved after allergen exposure)
- Skin/mucosa: hives, flushing, angioedema
- Respiratory: wheeze, stridor, dyspnea
- Cardiovascular: hypotension, tachycardia, syncope
- GI: cramping, vomiting

### Treatment — Do Not Delay Epinephrine
1. **EPINEPHRINE 0.3-0.5mg IM (1:1000 solution) in outer thigh** — first and most important step
2. Call 911
3. Position: supine with legs elevated (or semi-recumbent if respiratory distress)
4. Oxygen 10-15 L/min via non-rebreather mask
5. IV access — normal saline bolus 1-2L for hypotension
6. Second epinephrine dose in 5-15 minutes if no improvement
7. Diphenhydramine 50mg IV/IM (adjunct only — not for shock)
8. Methylprednisolone 125mg IV (prevents biphasic reaction)
9. Salbutamol nebulization for bronchospasm
10. Monitor minimum 4-6 hours (8-24 hours if severe)

### Discharge
- Prescribe epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) x2 doses
- Allergy referral
- Written anaphylaxis action plan
- Medical alert bracelet

## 🩸 Hypoglycemia Protocol

### Recognition
**Mild-Moderate**: Shakiness, sweating, palpitations, hunger, anxiety, pale skin, headache, blurred vision
**Severe**: Confusion, seizure, loss of consciousness, unable to self-treat

### Blood Glucose Thresholds
- Level 1: 54-70 mg/dL — treat immediately
- Level 2: <54 mg/dL — clinically significant, requires urgent treatment
- Level 3: Any glucose with altered mental status/seizure — severe

### Treatment — Rule of 15
1. **Conscious patient**: 15g fast-acting carbohydrates
   - 4 glucose tablets, OR
   - 4 oz (120mL) fruit juice/regular soda, OR
   - 1 tablespoon sugar/honey
2. Wait 15 minutes — recheck glucose
3. If still <70: repeat 15g carbohydrates
4. Once >70: eat small snack if meal not due

**Unconscious/unable to swallow**:
- Glucagon 1mg IM/SC (kit or nasal spray)
- Dextrose 50% 25g (50mL) IV push
- Recovery position
- Call 911 if no improvement in 10-15 minutes

## 🫁 Acute Asthma Protocol

### Severity Assessment
| Feature | Mild | Moderate | Severe | Life-threatening |
|---------|------|----------|--------|-----------------|
| SpO2 | ≥95% | 92-94% | <92% | <92% |
| PEF | >75% | 50-75% | 33-50% | <33% |
| Speech | Sentences | Phrases | Words | Cannot speak |
| Resp rate | Normal | 20-25 | >25 | >30 |

### Treatment
**All patients**: Salbutamol 2.5-5mg nebulized (or 4-8 puffs MDI via spacer) every 20 min x3

**Moderate-Severe**: Add
- Ipratropium bromide 0.5mg nebulized
- Systemic corticosteroids: prednisolone 40-50mg oral OR methylprednisolone 125mg IV
- Supplemental oxygen to keep SpO2 94-98%

**Severe/Life-threatening**: Add
- IV magnesium sulfate 2g over 20 minutes
- IV salbutamol if not responding to nebulized
- Heliox if available
- Prepare for intubation

## 💉 Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) Protocol

### Diagnostic Criteria
- Blood glucose >250 mg/dL (may be lower in euglycemic DKA)
- pH <7.3 or bicarbonate <15 mEq/L
- Anion gap >12
- Positive serum or urine ketones

### Management (4 Pillars)
1. **Fluids**: 0.9% NaCl 1L over first hour, then 500mL/hour x2h, then 250mL/hour
2. **Insulin**: Regular insulin 0.1 units/kg/hour IV infusion (bolus NOT recommended); start when K+ >3.5
3. **Potassium**: Replace to keep K+ 3.5-5.0 mEq/L; give 20-40 mEq/hour if K+ 3.5-5.5 and making urine
4. **Monitor**: Glucose hourly, electrolytes q2h, pH q2h; switch to dextrose when glucose <200

### Resolution Criteria
- pH >7.3 AND bicarbonate >15 AND anion gap ≤12
- Transition to subcutaneous insulin before stopping IV (overlap 2 hours)
